Transcript pane options

Same meeting page, four treatments of the transcript. Everything above the Transcript label is unchanged. Use the tabs to flip between them; the app frame scrolls on its own, so scroll down inside it to reach the transcript. Option 0 is what ships today, for comparison.

Current

Rows sit straight on the background render, same surface as the page controls. That is why the transcript reads as app copy rather than as a record you brought in.

What the options change

  • All four move the transcript onto solid black, your call.
  • They differ in how the text is set: same rows, prose, log, or a full width sheet.
  • Tools bar (In order / By speaker, Copy, Download) rides on the black in every option.

A · Pane

The smallest change. Same row structure as today (time, speaker, text), lifted into a solid black box with a hairline edge and 24px inside padding. The tools bar becomes the pane's header strip. The page keeps its width; the pane ends where the content column ends.

Read

  • Cheapest to build: one container, no new row types.
  • Black against the render already says "this is content, not chrome".
  • Playing row highlight stays as it is (white edge, faint fill).

B · Document

The transcript is set like a printed record. Speaker name runs in above each turn as a small semibold lead, timestamp sits in a mono gutter to the left, the text is one size up (16px) at a 640px measure with looser leading. No row dividers. Ends with a word count and engine line, like a colophon.

Read

  • Most legible for actually reading a meeting back.
  • Furthest from "UI text": reads as a document inside the app.
  • Play-from-here stays on the timestamp; the hover play glyph survives.

C · Readout

The transcript as data. Everything inside the pane is IBM Plex Mono: a column header (Time, Voice, Said), uppercase speaker labels, 13px text. Consistent with the design rule that Mono is for data and Sans is for what the app says to you. The transcript is the machine's output, so it takes the machine's face.

Read

  • Strongest separation from the app's own voice.
  • Denser: more turns per screen than A or B.
  • Long turns in mono get heavy; best if most meetings have short exchanges.

D · Sheet

Full bleed. The black runs edge to edge across the content area and down to the bottom, so the page is two zones: the meeting's controls on the render above, the record on black below. The player strip docks inside the sheet when audio plays. Row structure as today.

Read

  • Boldest of the four and closest to the "solid black sidebar" logic: a hard surface, not a card.
  • Scrolling down feels like moving from the app into the record.
  • Lens result cards above stay on the render, so results and record are visibly different things.
