SigmaFlow in the browser

Picks, 12 Nov 2026: 01 02 04 07 09. Built the same day.

Asked 12 Nov 2026: build a browser extension for two things the Mac app can't do in a browser today. 1. Learn from your fixes, and keep the saved transcript in step with them. Browsers don't let Accessibility read their text boxes, so in Gmail, Cliq on the web or any help desk the learner never fires. 2. Let Autowrite read the whole conversation as text, every email in the thread and not just the part a screenshot catches.

The extension works out of sight, so it needs very little design. These are the four places it can be seen, with 10 options across them. Reply with the numbers you want, one or more from each group, for example "01 05 07 09". Options in one group fight for the same spot, so pick one there unless the note says they combine. Names and sites are made up, and the browser is drawn as Dia.

From Mobbin: apps with a desktop app and an extension list each one with its own Add button: Grammarly's Apps page, Toggl's Browser extensions tab and Perplexity's three download tiles. n8n's Browser Use checks whether the extension is there and turns the row to "connected" by itself. 1Password asks at the moment you'd miss it. Matter makes it one step of setup, then asks you to pin the extension. Arcade puts a small card under its toolbar icon. n8n asks before it reads any tab.

    Already decided: fixes in a browser look like fixes anywhere else

    Point 1 adds no screen of its own. Fix a word SigmaFlow typed into Gmail, and the same correction card shows above the Dock as it does in Mail: the bar runs, then ADDED with Undo, and the Dictionary shows the rule as LEARNED. The saved transcript follows the edit the same way. The extension only gives SigmaFlow the text of that one box, so all four guardrails stay as they are. Drawn once for reference.

    A. How you add it

    01A card on Settings, Connections

    Under the Claude connections, a card lists the browsers on this Mac, each with its own Add. Add opens the browser's store page. Once the extension says hello, the row turns to Connected by itself, with no step to confirm. This is where privacy is said, once, in the card's footer. Mobbin: Grammarly, n8n.

    02Asked the first time you dictate into a browser

    The moment it matters: you've just dictated into Dia. A card shows above the Dock, once per browser and never again. SigmaFlow can't see a fix in a browser yet, so "the first dictation into a browser" is the only moment it can know about. Combines with 01, which stays the place to find it later. Mobbin: 1Password.

    03A step in the first open

    After the Meetings step, one more: "Your browser too". It only shows when a browser is installed. The two tiles show the before and after rather than describe them. Skip leaves 01 to find later. Combines with 01. Mobbin: Matter, Perplexity.

    B. Which sites it can read

    This choice decides the browser's warning at install, which SigmaFlow can't change or style. Either way, the extension reads only the box SigmaFlow just typed into, and the page only while the Autowrite key is held. What it reads goes straight to SigmaFlow on this Mac and nowhere else.

    04Every site, from the start

    One warning at install, the browser's own words: "Read and change all your data on all websites". After that, learning and Autowrite work on every site with nothing more to click. The words are scary next to "100% privacy", but they show once. Mobbin: ChatGPT's connector install asks the same "all or some" question.

    05Each site, allowed by you

    The install warning is milder ("Communicate with cooperating native applications"), and the extension reads nothing until you allow a site. Browsers only let an extension ask after a click on it, so the first time you dictate on a new site, SigmaFlow's toolbar icon gets a dot. You click it and allow that site. More clicks, but each site is your choice, and the extension needs pinning to the toolbar to be seen. Mobbin: n8n.

    C. What its toolbar icon does

    06Nothing to click

    The extension stays in the browser's extensions menu and isn't pinned. Clicking it opens SigmaFlow on Settings, Connections. It has no card of its own and no settings: one place to change things, the app. Works with 04, not with 05 (05 needs a card to allow sites in).

    07A small card with one switch for this site

    Pinned, its icon opens a card: SigmaFlow, Connected, and one switch that turns it off on the site you're on. Banking, a password manager's page, anything you'd rather it left alone. Works with 04 and 05; with 05, the switch reads Allow until you've allowed the site. Mobbin: Arcade.

    08A card with a switch for each, and what it learned here

    The same card, but learning and Autowrite are switched on this site one at a time, and the last fixes learned on this site are listed under them. It shows the extension doing something, not just sitting there. More to read, and the list repeats what the Dictionary already shows.

    D. When Autowrite reads a page

    09The overlay names what it read

    Today a small screenshot sits beside your words. With the extension, the screenshot gives way to what was actually read: the site, the ticket or subject, and how much ("all 6 emails"), so you know it saw the part scrolled out of view. Where the extension can't read (another app, or a site you turned off), the screenshot comes back as today.

    10The spotlight lights the part it read

    Today the spotlight dims everything but the front window. In a browser it can narrow to the conversation it read and the box it will write into, with a thin white edge, drawn by the extension inside the page. It shows exactly where the writing comes from, for as long as the key is held. Combines with 09.
