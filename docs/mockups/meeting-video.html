    A. What gets filmed

    01The call's window

    Only the window of the app holding the microphone: Zoom, Teams, or the browser that has Meet open. It is filmed even while another window covers it, and a notification over it never gets in. It follows the window to another screen. Moving to another tab in that same browser films the other tab. A minimised window films nothing until it is back. A meeting started by hand with no call app holding the mic has no window to film, so it is sound only.

    02The whole screen the pointer is on

    The same thing live notes' screenshots take: everything on that screen, SigmaFlow left out. The simplest, and it works with no call at all (a demo, a slide deck, an in-person meeting with a screen). It also films every notification, chat and private window that crosses the screen while you record.

    Strain, asked 4 Nov: the picture costs next to nothing. It is a copy of a frame the recording already has, shrunk to 192 × 120 once a second, and only while the Meetings page is on screen. The recording itself is the real cost. On the M4, ScreenCaptureKit hands frames over in graphics memory and the encoding runs on the chip's media engine, not the processor, so I expect a few percent of CPU and a small battery cost at 2880 × 1800, 30 frames a second. I have not measured that yet. The shell can't record the screen, so I'd measure it from the app on a real call before 13 is built, and leave it out if the numbers say otherwise.

    Measured 5 Nov (SigmaFlow --video-check 30 FOLDER, the built recorder, the whole 3024 × 1964 screen at 30 frames a second, the stills check running): SigmaFlow 3.3% of one core, the encoder 0 to 1.3%, a mostly still screen 0.9 GB an hour (2 GB at most, the bit rate's ceiling). The stills check already shrinks one frame a second, as 13 would, and is inside that 3.3%.

    E. How sharp

    Sizes are estimates for a call with shared slides, using the Mac's hardware encoder. I'd measure them on a real call before building.

    18720p, 10 frames a second, about 0.2 GB an hour

    Faces are soft and a slide's headings are readable, but its small print is not. A year of weekly hour-long meetings comes to about 10 GB.

    191080p, 15 frames a second, about 0.5 GB an hour

    Slides and shared code can be read, and movement is smooth enough for faces. It matches the 1920 px that live notes' screenshots use. A year of weekly meetings comes to about 25 GB.

    20The screen's own size, 30 frames a second, about 2 GB an hourPICKED
